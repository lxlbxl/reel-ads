# Claims check before locking copy

Ad copy for a paid Reel/TikTok/Shorts placement is held to a different
standard than a brand's own website or an organic post. Run this check
before generating VO or finalizing on-screen text -- catching it after
audio is generated means paying to regenerate.

## What to look for

Scan the brand's site, deck, or provided materials for any number, stat,
or outcome claim, and check whether it's qualified as:
- "Illustrative Scenario" / "Illustrative Example"
- "Modeled outcome" / "Projected" / "Estimated"
- A placeholder, a demo figure, or explicitly hypothetical

If a claim carries any of those qualifiers, **do not** present it as a
real, unqualified result in ad copy or VO ("38% more booked jobs," "$180K
recovered," etc. stated flatly). Two problems, either of which is enough
to require a different approach:

1. **Regulatory**: an unqualified performance claim in paid advertising
   that the business can't substantiate is an FTC exposure issue in the
   US (and equivalent consumer-protection regimes elsewhere), regardless
   of who the client is or how the claim originated.
2. **Platform rejection**: Meta and TikTok ad review both flag
   unsubstantiated specific-outcome claims; a label like "Illustrative
   Scenario" that's clearly visible on the client's own site will not
   survive being lifted into a 20-second paid spot with no such
   qualifier attached.

## What to lead with instead

Prefer, in this order:
1. **A stated guarantee or contractual term** the business actually
   offers (a money-back guarantee, an implementation timeline promise, a
   response-time SLA). These are real commitments, not modeled outcomes,
   and they're usually a stronger hook than a vague stat anyway.
2. **A named, real offer** (a specific price, a specific package, a free
   trial/assessment).
3. **A generic capability claim** that doesn't imply a specific
   magnitude of result ("we build your AI receptionist and run it for
   you" vs. "cut missed calls by 40%").
4. **A clearly qualified claim**, if a stat must be used at all --
   "modeled outcome," "illustrative," or similar, kept visibly attached
   to the number, not dropped when the copy gets punchier.

## If you're not sure

If a number's status isn't clearly labeled one way or the other on the
source material, ask the person rather than assuming it's safe to use
flatly -- this is a case where guessing wrong costs more (a rejected ad
spend, or worse, real exposure) than a clarifying question does.

This check is a normal part of scripting the ad, not a separate
compliance step to bolt on afterward -- do it while drafting the script
in `vo_sync.md` step 1, before anything is generated.
